Ask ten designers what a logo costs and you’ll get ten answers, from “free” to “six figures”. They’re all right, because “a logo” can mean anything from a font choice to a fully researched identity system used by thousands of employees. The useful question is: what are you actually buying at each price, and what do you need right now?

What drives the price

  • Strategy. Research, workshops and positioning take time. Cheap logos skip this; expensive ones are mostly this.
  • Originality and craft. Custom lettering, a symbol drawn from scratch and careful optical adjustments take hours that templates don’t.
  • Scope of the system. A logo alone is small. Colour, typography, patterns, iconography, motion and templates multiply the work.
  • Usage and risk. A national retailer’s logo appears on millions of items. Mistakes are costly, so testing and legal clearance are more thorough.
  • Seniority and location. A senior designer in a high-cost city charges more per hour; you’re paying for judgement as well as hands.
  • Revisions and stakeholders. More decision-makers means more rounds and more cost.
  • Rights. Full copyright assignment should be standard, but confirm it in writing at every price point.

What “a logo” should include

Whatever you pay, insist on a minimum deliverable set:

  • Primary logo plus horizontal/stacked versions and a square icon.
  • Full-colour, one-colour (black) and reversed (white) versions.
  • Vector master files (SVG, AI or EPS) and PNGs with transparent backgrounds.
  • Exact colour values (HEX, RGB, CMYK; Pantone if you print).
  • Font names and licence information.
  • Basic usage rules: clear space, minimum size, misuses.
  • Written transfer of copyright to you.

Which level is right for you?

Pre-launch or testing an idea

Spend little. A clean wordmark in a good free font and a disciplined palette will serve you better than an elaborate symbol. Use our wordmark maker, palette builder and brand guide builder — then reinvest when you have traction.

Launched and growing

An experienced freelancer or small studio ($1,000–$15,000) is usually the sweet spot: real strategy, original work and a usable system, without agency overheads.

Raising capital, entering retail or rebranding

A studio or agency with category experience reduces risk. Expect research, multiple stakeholder sessions and a full rollout plan.

Red flags at any price

  1. No questions about your business before showing concepts.
  2. Portfolios full of work that looks borrowed from famous brands.
  3. Unlimited concepts in 24 hours for $20 — often recycled templates.
  4. No written agreement about ownership.
  5. Raster-only files (JPEG/PNG) with no vector master.
  6. Refusal to show process or reference real clients.

How to budget the whole brand, not just the logo

The logo is often a minority of the total cost of a brand launch. Plan for trademark filing (in the US the base federal filing fee is $350 per class since January 2025, before any surcharges or attorney fees), domain names, website design, photography, packaging or signage, and printed materials. A common rule of thumb is to allocate your identity budget first, then roughly double it for rollout across touchpoints.

Tip: fixed-price proposals with clearly defined deliverables and revision rounds protect both sides. Hourly billing suits open-ended strategy work.
