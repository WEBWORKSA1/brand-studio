Brand strategy has a reputation for being expensive and vague. It doesn’t have to be either. At its core, a brand strategy answers a short list of questions clearly enough that everyone in the business makes the same decisions. This template fits on one page.

The template

SectionQuestion to answerExample (fictional coffee roaster)
PurposeWhy do we exist beyond making money?To make the everyday coffee ritual something to look forward to.
AudienceWho exactly do we serve first?Home brewers, 25–45, who care about taste but don’t want to be coffee nerds.
ProblemWhat frustrates them today?Specialty coffee feels intimidating; supermarket coffee tastes flat.
PositioningFor whom, what, and why us?The specialty roaster for people who don’t want a lecture — great coffee, plain-English guides.
PersonalityWho are we at a dinner party?Everyday Friend with a touch of Creator: warm, curious, unpretentious.
VoiceThree words, “this not that”.Warm not cute; knowledgeable not snobby; simple not dumbed-down.
Messaging pillarsThree things we always communicate.Roasted fresh weekly · Easy to brew well · Fairly sourced.
ProofWhy should anyone believe us?Roast date on every bag; brewing videos; named farm partners.
Signature assetsWhat will people recognise us by?Terracotta colour, hand-drawn bean icon, “Brew it your way” sign-off.
MeasuresHow will we know it’s working?Repeat-purchase rate, branded search, reviews mentioning “easy”.

How to fill it in

Purpose

Keep it human and specific. If any company in your category could claim it, push further.

Audience

Choose a primary audience narrow enough to describe vividly. You can widen later; brands that try to appeal to everyone from day one tend to appeal to no one.

Positioning

Use the classic structure: For [audience] who [need], [Brand] is the [category] that [benefit]. Unlike [alternative], we [difference], because [proof].

Personality and voice

Pick an archetype as a shortcut — the quiz takes two minutes — then define three voice words with their “not” counterparts.

Messaging pillars

Three is the right number. Each should be a customer benefit you can prove.

Proof

Concrete evidence beats adjectives: numbers, guarantees, processes, named partners, reviews.

Signature assets

Decide which elements you’ll repeat until they’re unmistakably yours — a colour, a shape, a phrase, a sound.

Measures

Pick two or three indicators you can actually track. Review quarterly.

Test your strategy

  • Can a new employee explain the positioning after reading it once?
  • Would your best customer recognise themselves in the audience description?
  • Does it rule anything out? A strategy that excludes nothing isn’t a strategy.
  • Can every pillar be proven today?

From strategy to identity

With strategy settled, identity decisions become much easier. Move on to the palette, fonts and wordmark, then capture everything in the brand guide builder. If you need facilitation, a brand strategist can run this in a half-day workshop.
