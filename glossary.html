Reference

Branding glossary

The vocabulary of branding, without the jargon. Search or filter by topic.

Brand

The set of perceptions people hold about a company, product or person. You influence it; customers ultimately decide it.

Branding

The ongoing work of shaping those perceptions through strategy, identity, communication and experience.

Brand architecture

How a company organises its brands and sub-brands — for example one master brand (“branded house”) versus many independent brands (“house of brands”).

Brand equity

The commercial value created by people’s awareness of and feelings about a brand — why customers choose or pay more for it.

Brand positioning

The distinct place a brand aims to occupy in the customer’s mind relative to alternatives.

Positioning statement

An internal sentence: for [audience], [brand] is the [category] that [benefit] because [reason to believe].

Value proposition

The specific benefit a customer gets, stated in their terms.

Brand promise

The experience customers can expect every time. Breaking it damages trust faster than any campaign can build it.

Brand archetype

One of twelve character models (Hero, Sage, Jester…) used to define personality. Take the quiz.

Brand pillars

Three to five core themes or values that all messaging ladders up to.

Differentiation

What makes a brand meaningfully different from competitors, ideally in a way customers care about.

Distinctiveness

How easily a brand is recognised — via consistent colours, shapes, characters, sounds or phrases — regardless of whether it is “different”.

Distinctive brand assets

Non-name elements that trigger recall of a brand, such as a colour, jingle or mascot.

Rebrand

A significant change to a brand’s strategy, name or identity. A “refresh” is a lighter update to visuals.

Visual identity

The visible system of a brand: logo, colour, typography, imagery, iconography and layout.

Wordmark (logotype)

A logo made of the brand name in distinctive type. Make one.

Lettermark (monogram)

A logo made of initials, useful for long names.

Brandmark (symbol)

A pictorial or abstract symbol used with or without the name.

Combination mark

A symbol and wordmark used together, often with defined lockups.

Emblem

A logo where text sits inside a symbol or badge, common in education, sport and heritage brands.

Lockup

A fixed arrangement of logo elements (e.g. horizontal or stacked) that shouldn’t be rearranged.

Clear space

The minimum empty area around a logo that other elements must not enter.

Responsive logo

A family of logo versions that simplify as space shrinks — from full lockup down to favicon.

Brand guidelines

The rulebook for applying a brand consistently. Build a one-page guide.

Vector

Artwork defined by mathematical paths (SVG, AI, EPS) that scales without losing quality — the right format for logo masters.

Typeface vs font

A typeface is the design (e.g. a family); a font is a specific weight and style file within it.

Serif / sans-serif

Serif faces have small strokes at letter ends; sans-serifs don’t.

Kerning

Adjusting space between specific pairs of letters. Critical in wordmarks.

Tracking

Uniform spacing applied across a whole word or line.

X-height

The height of lowercase letters; affects how large and readable a font feels.

HEX / RGB / CMYK

Colour codes for screen (HEX, RGB) and four-colour print (CMYK).

Pantone (PMS)

A proprietary spot-colour matching system used for consistent print and product colours.

Contrast ratio

The luminance difference between text and background. WCAG AA asks for 4.5:1 for body text. Check yours.

Colour harmony

Rules for combining hues — analogous, complementary, triadic and others.

Trademark

A legally protected sign — word, logo, slogan, sometimes colour or sound — that identifies the source of goods or services.

™ vs ®

™ claims rights in a mark (usable anywhere unregistered); ® may only be used once the mark is registered in that country.

Nice Classification

The international system of 45 classes (34 goods, 11 services) used to define what a trademark covers.

Likelihood of confusion

The core test for trademark conflicts: would consumers likely confuse the source, given similar marks and related goods?

Madrid Protocol

A WIPO system for seeking trademark protection in many member countries through one application.

Descriptive mark

A mark that merely describes the product (e.g. “Fast Delivery”). Hard to register without proof of acquired distinctiveness.

Copyright

Protects original creative works such as logo artwork. Ownership of a commissioned logo should be assigned to you in writing.

Brand awareness

How many people in your market recognise or recall your brand.

Brand voice

The consistent personality expressed in words; tone flexes by situation, voice doesn’t.

Boilerplate

A standard short description of the company used in press releases and profiles.

Touchpoint

Any place a person encounters your brand — packaging, website, support call, invoice.
